# Circles and Levels

## How individual and organizational intelligence levels operate inside each substrate loop

*2026-08-23*
*Crosses: `SARSI-L_Paper_v3.md` (Circles I–V+) × `Two_Axis_Intelligence_Level_Framework_v2.md` (I0–IΩ, O0–OΩ)*

---

## § 1 — What the crossing asks

SARSI-L indexes recursive self-improvement by **substrate**: software, hardware, physical, biological,
stellar. The two-axis framework indexes it by **who changes what**: an individual's write depth, and
an organization's coordination depth. The two have never been crossed, and the crossing is not
decorative — it produces a result neither framework states.

The bridge is SARSI-L's own loop. Every circle is $L_d = \langle \iota, \mu, \nu, \delta \rangle$:
identify a candidate modification, implement it, validate it against an evaluation not used in
generating it, deploy it as the operative baseline. **Each of those four steps is performed by an
individual or by an organization, and which one it is changes systematically as the circle index
rises.**

> **The assignment.** ι is individual work — generating a candidate is cognition.
> μ is individual in Circle I and increasingly physical and multi-actor outward.
> **ν is organizational by necessity**, since validation requires an evaluator the proposer did not
> author. **δ is organizational always** — no individual can authorize its own deployment without
> the promotion becoming self-certification.

That last clause is the whole result in miniature. Two of the four steps of *every* loop, in *every*
circle, are organizational by construction. No individual level closes any circle alone.

## § 2 — The general law

> **The individual requirement saturates; the organizational requirement does not.**

Reading SARSI-L's five closure criteria (§3.2) against the level definitions:

| Circle | ι demands | μ, ν, δ demand | Binding axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** Software | I3–I4 | O2–O3 | **O** (authorization, per §3.3) |
| **II** Hardware | I2–I3 | O3–O4 + physical | **O**, then physical |
| **III** Physical | I2–I3 | O4–O5 + physical | **Physical**, via O |
| **IV** Biological | **I5** | O3–O5 + irreducible physical | Physical (5–8 yr endpoints) |
| **V+** Stellar | I2–I3 | O4+ | **O**, and no individual is possible |

*Estimates by inspection, in the corpus's idiom — orderings, not measurements (§10.3).*

Three things follow.

**You cannot buy an outer circle with a smarter individual.** The ι column stops rising after Circle
IV and is *lower* in II, III, and V+ than in I. Chip specification, machine design, and collector
design are ordinary engineering cognition; what is hard about those circles is everything after ι.

**The intelligence-explosion argument is a Circle I argument.** Good's machine improves its own
cognition: that is I3 → I4, an individual-axis claim. It is silent about circles whose binding steps
are organizational and physical. This is SARSI-L's own thesis — recursion is substrate-indexed —
supplied with a mechanism it does not state: *substrate matters because outer substrates shift the
load from the individual axis to the organizational one.*

**The design/deploy gap in §3.3 is the I/O gap.** SARSI-L observes that in every row the design
component is far above the manufacture/deploy component, and calls the gap "institutional as often as
technical." In level terms every row reads **I high, O low**, and that single diagnosis covers all
five substrate domains at once.

## § 3 — Circle by circle

### Circle I — Software

**What each level does.** I0 executors run the evaluations and the mechanical parts of the pipeline.
I1 operators hold session and project state so an experiment survives a restart. I2 specialists tune
policy against a fixed metric. **I3 is the level Circle I's criterion actually names** — a system that
modifies its own architecture or training process — and I4 is what makes the loop recursive rather
than merely closed. On the organizational side, O1 retains what was tried and what failed, O2 routes
candidates to the right evaluator, and **O3 is where the loop's own validation procedure becomes
improvable**.

**Where it is stuck, precisely.** ι, μ, ν run without approval in mature systems; δ does not. SARSI-L
§3.3 says the constraint on (d) is authorization, not capability. In level terms: **the software loop
has an I-level roughly adequate to its criterion and an O-level roughly two steps short.**

**So "authority closure" is an O-axis move, not an I-axis one.** The companion paper's identity —
that ρ_max = 1/T_δ and authority closure is the removal of δ from the gated set — is precisely the
claim that Circle I closure is achieved by raising O, not I. Capability closure drives ι, μ, ν toward
zero and cannot touch T_δ.

**Floor type: throughput.** Removable. This is the only circle of which that is true across the board.

### Circle II — Hardware

**Where the axes split.** SARSI-L's own critical distinction — that (a)–(b) chip *design* is a
software problem and (c)–(d) *manufacture* is physical infrastructure — is exactly the I/O split. The
design half is I2–I3 and approaching maturity. The manufacture half is a building, a supply chain,
contamination control, and process-drift correction at nanometre tolerances: **coordination among
many actors over months, which is O-work by definition and cannot be performed by any individual.**

**This is where the individual axis stops helping.** A more capable designer does not qualify a fab.
The circle's binding step is unreachable from the I-axis at any level, and SARSI-L's report that
conflating design with manufacture "was the single most consequential error in this framework's
earlier timelines" is the same error stated in substrate terms.

**Minimum: I2–I3 / O3–O4.** O3 because the organization must be able to change its own process
control on evidence; O4 because doing that repeatedly and getting better at it is what fab autonomy
would mean.

### Circle III — Physical

**Self-replication is an organizational property.** The criterion is that a machine mine, refine,
fabricate its own control electronics, and assemble a working copy. Every step after design is
distributed over space and time. And the copy raises a question the I-axis cannot answer and the
O-axis can: *is the copy the same system?* By the lineage criterion it is a legitimate successor iff
the replication is recorded with its provenance — which makes self-replication a **lineage** event, not
merely a manufacturing one.

**Minimum: I2–I3 / O4–O5.** The ι is machine design; the rest is an industrial organization.

**Floor type: physical.** Not removable. Parallelism is the only lever, and by SARSI-L §5.5 the
capability that supplies parallelism in matter *is* Circle III — the ladder must be climbed to get the
tool that would make the ladder fast.

**The II↔III bootstrap is an organizational ladder.** SARSI-L's Proposition 3 shows the mutual
dependency is a bootstrap rather than a deadlock, advancing at the slower of the two domains' physical
periods. In level terms both rungs are O-work; **no increase in individual level moves the ladder at
all.** That is why it advances at physical speed.

### Circle IV — Biological

**The only circle whose ι genuinely requires I5.** Its criterion opens with "generates and tests
hypotheses about biological mechanisms" — autonomous identification of significant unknowns, which is
exactly the autonomous-discovery level. Every other circle's ι is design, satisfied at I2–I3.

**And its ν is irreducibly external, twice over.** Once because validation always needs a separated
evaluator, and once because the regulatory structure requires a human principal investigator — an
institutional fact, not a technical one. Its physical floor of five to eight years is not compressible
by any level on either axis.

**The irony worth stating.** SARSI-L §5.3 shows Circle IV is off the critical path: it requires
Circle I, does not require Circle III, and Circle V+ does not require it. So **the circle that most
demands a high individual level is the one that contributes least to the trajectory.** The top of the
I-axis is stranded on a branch.

### Circle V+ — Stellar

**No individual is possible here, and this is the sharpest cross-result.** A swarm at 1 AU is roughly
16.6 light-minutes across. By the coherence bound $R \le c\tau/2$, a single decision locus of that
radius has a cycle of at least 16.6 minutes — and the local computation available between consecutive
opportunities to agree is enormous. Such a system is not a mind with distributed parts; it is a
civilization. **Circle V+ has no I-coordinate. It is purely an O-problem, by physics.**

**Minimum: O4 at least.** The criterion's net-growth condition — collection area rising faster than
maintenance and failure losses, sustained — requires an organization that improves how it improves,
over decades. Whether it requires OΩ specifically is not entailed by the letter of the criterion, and
we do not claim it; what is entailed is that no fixed organizational form is obviously adequate to
the timescale, which is OΩ's characteristic condition.

## § 4 — Two asymmetries at the top

Checking the level requirements against SARSI-L §3.2, which is the only place closure is defined:

> **No circle's closure criterion requires IΩ.**

Circle I needs I3–I4. Circles II, III, V+ need I2–I3 for their design steps. Circle IV needs I5.
Open-ended individual intelligence — discoveries becoming cognitive tools that expand one's own
future discovery space — is required by **none** of the five criteria. IΩ is not on the path to
Kardashev II.

> **The outermost circle is unreachable by any individual at all.**

By §3's coherence argument, Circle V+ admits no individual coordinate. The organizational axis has no
counterpart bound: an organization may span any distance, because it was never required to hold one
coherent state.

Together: **the individual axis has a ceiling and terminates below the trajectory's requirements,
while the organizational axis carries the outer circles by itself.** This is the two-axis framework's
asymmetry result — that organization is the more fundamental coordinate — derived a second time from
an independent starting point, which is the strongest reason to believe it.

It also gives IΩ a clearer status. Not that IΩ is unimportant; that it is **not instrumental**. If IΩ
is worth pursuing, it is worth pursuing for what it is, and not as a step toward stellar-scale
capability, because the stellar-scale path does not pass through it.

## § 5 — What this explains in SARSI-L

**The half-circle, defined on the level map.** SARSI-L Definition 4 says a half-circle has one or more
steps performed by an agent external to the system. In level terms: **a half-circle is a system whose
I-level meets its circle's requirement and whose O-level does not.** Compensation is an organization
supplying the O-work the system cannot. That makes the compensation-ceiling crux (§4.4–4.5)
measurable: H₁ versus H₂ turns on whether the O-gap closes as the compensating organization is itself
augmented — which is the T_δ question, and T_δ is measurable from timestamps.

**Why the framework's own errors were monotonic in substrate distance.** §5.6 reports that timeline
corrections grew with distance from software, and attributes it to reasoning about physical domains
with software intuitions. The level crossing sharpens this: distance from software is distance from
the I-axis. **The framework underestimated in proportion to how much of each circle was O-work and
physical work rather than cognition** — and an analysis calibrated on the individual axis will
mis-estimate exactly in that proportion.

**Why λ's weights are not arbitrary.** §10.3 records that the per-step weights $w_i$ are estimated by
inspection and that two loops with identical λ can differ enormously depending on which steps were
automated. The crossing offers a principled decomposition: weight ι and μ by the I-axis and ν and δ by
the O-axis, and report λ as a pair rather than a scalar. Two loops with the same λ but different I/O
splits are then distinguishable, which is what §10.3 asks for.

**Why the dominance model's "floor" term is the one good idea in it.** §7 keeps the floor — inner
loops never go to zero — and discards the thirty fitted parameters. The crossing explains the floor:
inner loops persist because their I-work and O-work remain necessary inputs to outer loops. Circle I's
cognition is required by every outer circle's ι, forever.

## § 6 — What to measure

Three quantities, in order of cost, all following from the crossing.

**T_δ per circle.** Authorization latency is the O-axis's binding quantity in every circle. Measured
once, on a deployed governed software system: **never observed**, and its system of record has no
timestamp field. Circle I's δ is the only δ currently instrumentable at all — the outer circles' δ
runs through procurement, regulators, and launch authorities, where the timestamps exist but are not
collected as a series.

**The I/O split of λ.** Recompute SARSI-L §3.3's table with each step tagged by axis. The prediction
is that the O-tagged fraction is near zero in every row, which would make the table's "design vs
manufacture" columns a special case of a general rule rather than a domain observation.

**Whether the ι-requirement really saturates.** §2's table is the crossing's central claim and is an
ordering by inspection. It would be refuted by a circle whose identify step demonstrably requires a
higher individual level than Circle IV's, or by any closure criterion that turns out to require IΩ.

## § 7 — Limitations

**The level assignments are estimates by inspection.** They inherit §10.3's problem exactly: no agreed
decomposition of each loop into steps, no agreed weighting, and levels that are ordinal on both axes.
The table in §2 is an ordering.

**The ι/μ/ν/δ-to-axis assignment is cleaner in the statement than in practice.** ν in Circle I is
partly individual — a system can run its own held-out evaluation, and what makes it organizational is
that the *criterion and data* must be external, not the execution. The assignment is about where the
authority lies, not where the compute runs, and that distinction does real work in Circle I and less
outward.

**"No circle requires IΩ" is a claim about the stated criteria.** SARSI-L §3.2 is the only place
closure is defined, and the claim is checked against it. If the criteria are incomplete — and §2.4
concedes the domain list is "neither obviously complete nor obviously minimal" — a sixth circle could
require IΩ. The most-discussed candidate, fault-tolerant quantum computation, would not.

**Circle V+'s O-requirement is bounded below, not identified.** We argue O4 at least, and decline to
claim OΩ, because the criterion does not entail it. That leaves the outermost circle's organizational
requirement genuinely open.
