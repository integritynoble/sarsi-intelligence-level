# AI-Level: From LLM to Dyson Sphere

*The Five Circles of Recursive Self-Improvement, and the Grand Problems Between Us and a Type II Civilization*

Book outline, version 0 — 6 September 2026. For revision before any chapter is drafted.

## Title

**Recommended:** *AI-Level: From LLM to Dyson Sphere* — subtitle *The Five Circles of Recursive Self-Improvement, and the Grand Problems Between Us and a Type II Civilization*.

Alternatives:

- *Five Circles: The AI-Level Road from Language Models to a Type II Civilization*
- *Centering Outward: AI-Level, Substrate Circles, and the Problems Each Generation Must Solve*

The working phrase "from LLM to Type II civilization Dyson sphere" is right in content. "Dyson Sphere" belongs in the main title (concrete, memorable); "Type II civilization" belongs in the subtitle, where it can be defined.

## Thesis

An LLM is an I0 individual at the entrance of Circle I. A Dyson sphere is the closure of Circle V+. Between them lie five substrate circles — software, hardware, matter, biology, stellar — each defined not by how clever the intelligence is but by which substrate its improvement loop (identify → implement → validate → deploy) can close over. AI-Level theory says what kind of intelligence each step needs; circle theory says which substrate the step must reach.

Two facts organize the book:

1. **Validate and deploy are organizational in every circle**, so no individual intelligence closes any loop alone.
2. **The individual axis saturates while the organizational axis keeps climbing**, so the outer circles are won by organizations, not minds.

A civilization *centers* into the next circle when the current loop closes and the binding constraint migrates outward. Each circle has a short list of grand problems; the book names them, says which AI-Level each needs, and calls the people of each circle's time to work on them.

## Part I — Two Ladders, One Road (theory)

1. **What "level" means, and why one number fails.** Capability, persistence, self-modification, coordination and substrate reach are five different questions. The profile, not the scalar.
2. **AI-Level.** The coordinates [C, I, O, T, H, SA]; memory M as a one-way prerequisite; U0–UΩ as a gated ladder; levels as invariant contrasts, never difficulty thresholds; typed cumulativity (retention on capability ladders, a frontier for delegation, nesting for harnesses, none on the T and H axes).
3. **The circles.** Circle I software, II hardware, III physical matter and self-reproduction, IV biology, V+ stellar. A circle is what substrate the loop reaches. Closure criteria, stated to the step.
4. **The loop.** Identify, implement, validate, deploy — assigned to axes. Why validate and deploy are organizational by construction; the self-certification trap; compensated half-loops (humans supplying the missing steps, which is where every current system lives).
5. **The two asymmetries.** The individual requirement saturates at I3–I5; the organizational requirement climbs to OΩ. The coherence bound: a structure spanning an astronomical unit is eight orders of magnitude beyond any single mind, so Circle V+ has no individual coordinate at all.

## Part II — The Circles (the heart)

Each circle chapter has the same shape: *closure target · where we stand today (measured) · the grand problems · the key technologies · which AI-Levels each problem needs · what centering into the next circle looks like · the call to this circle's generation.*

### 6. Circle 0 — The LLM

The bare model measured through a harness: I0, M0, O0 by construction; C and SA readable in minutes. Why a language model is the entrance, not the road. The three measured index rows as the baseline.

### 7. Circle I — Software

**Closure:** a system that modifies its own architecture or training, validates on held-out evaluation, and *deploys* without a human in the modal loop.

**Grand problems**

1. Persistence that survives restart with provenance (I1 / M1).
2. Learning from verified experience that transfers to unseen tasks (I2 / M3).
3. Governed self-improvement — a declared mechanism Θ, sealed evaluation, promoter separation (I3).
4. Recursive improvement of the improvement process Ψ (I4).
5. Autonomous discovery with an incorporation gate (I5).
6. Verification the system cannot write to — computed keys, false-completion pricing, the FCR/FRR pair.
7. Organizations of agents with real separation of roles (O2–O3), because validate and deploy are theirs.
8. The measurement problem itself — invariant levels and a non-saturating index, so we can tell when a problem is solved.

**Technologies:** harness generations HG1–HG4, memory architectures M1–M5, sealed-suite evaluation, organizational runtimes, AI-Level Bench and the AI-Level Index.

### 8. Circle II — Hardware

**Closure:** autonomous chip specification, production-ready design, fabrication at viable yield, integration into the system's own compute.

**Grand problems**

1. End-to-end autonomous chip design (informational; I3–I5 plus O2).
2. The autonomous fab — process control and yield learning as an improvement loop at physical timescales.
3. Running on chips it designed (the deploy step, organizational).
4. Compute per watt and cooling as the real ceiling.
5. The design-to-manufacture gap, which reappears in every outer circle.

**Technologies:** EDA agents, fab digital twins, closed-loop metrology, robotic fabs.

### 9. Bridge — Circle II and Circle III bootstrap each other

Autonomous fabs need physical automation; self-reproducing machines need electronics. Neither closes first. Circle status is a vector, not a stage number.

### 10. Circle III — Matter and Self-Reproduction

**Closure:** identify a physical need, design and manufacture machines from available materials, operate under unforeseen failure, repair and reproduce from in-situ resources.

**Grand problems**

1. General-purpose autonomous manufacturing from raw materials — the constructor that builds constructors.
2. Dexterous manipulation and repair under failures no one anticipated (embodied I2–I3).
3. The matter bottleneck — transport, materials, qualification, failure physics: floors that cognition cannot compress.
4. Verifying physical outcomes with instruments the system does not control.
5. Autonomous logistics and materials loops.

**Technologies:** industrial and humanoid robotics, additive manufacturing, closed-loop materials, digital twins of plants.

### 11. Circle IV — Biology (in parallel, not on the critical path)

**Closure:** autonomous hypothesis generation and testing, intervention design, validation through experimental and clinical pathways, population-scale deployment and iteration.

**Grand problems**

1. Self-driving laboratories that close identify → validate.
2. Intervention design with clinical endpoints as irreducible physical time.
3. The incorporation gate for biological discoveries (the I5 test, with the cancer campaign as the worked example).
4. Population-scale deployment under governance and consent.
5. Understanding the biological mind — the coherence bound from the inside.

**Why it runs in parallel:** it needs Circle I cognition and is not a prerequisite for Circle V+.

### 12. Circle V+ — Stellar

**Closure:** a self-expanding orbital manufacturing loop that builds collectors and uses the captured energy to grow net-positive collection area — the Dyson swarm as a loop, not a structure.

**Grand problems**

1. Net-positive self-expansion in orbit.
2. In-situ resource acquisition off-planet.
3. Energy capture, transmission and use at stellar scale.
4. Organization across light-latency — O4–OΩ coordination where no individual coordinate exists.
5. Autonomy and repair over decades (Circle III in vacuum).
6. The governance of a civilization-scale actor.

**Technologies:** launch economics, orbital manufacturing, autonomous swarms, power beaming, coordination protocols under latency.

### 13. Kardashev, measured

Type I ≈ Circles I–IV closed on a planet; Type II ≈ Circle V+ closed; Type III beyond this book. Energy capture is an *indicator* of civilizational level and never its definition — the same rule that keeps the AI-Level from being an energy score. What "centering into" a circle means operationally: the binding constraint has migrated outward and the previous circle's loop runs without compensation.

## Part III — Knowing When a Problem Is Solved

14. **Transition tests.** For every I, O and circle transition, the required evidence inside the circle: contrasts with control arms, retention, external evaluators, promoter separation. Why a claim without its test is a description.
15. **Anti-inflation.** Ten I1 agents in parallel are still O1; a self-grading redesign pipeline is O1 plus an unverified claim; adding agents does not move a system outward. The compensated half-loop as the honest label for today.
16. **The instrument.** AI-Level Bench and the index as the civilization's ruler: invariant levels, refreshed witnesses, bands not ranks, frozen anchors — so the ruler does not move while the road is walked.

## Part IV — People, Organizations, Time

17. **Why organizations carry the outer circles.** Separation forces heterogeneous levels; timescale forces heterogeneous write-depths; physical bounds force heterogeneous designs; lower levels become *more* valuable as compression rises. The seven-role loop: director, discoverer, instrument-maker, specialist, operator, referee, promoter.
18. **Circle time.** Each circle's characteristic timescale — days, months, years, decades, centuries — and what it means to be the generation of a circle.
19. **The call.** Where we are: early Circle I, I1 and O0–O1, with humans supplying validate and deploy. What to work on now, in order: close Circle I honestly; build the II–III bootstrap prerequisites; run Circle IV in parallel; prepare the organizational forms Circle V+ will demand. Who should work on what, and why the referee is the scarcest role.
20. **Risks and refusals.** Self-certification, inflation, energy-as-intelligence, the coherence bound ignored, deployment authority captured. What the framework refuses to count.
